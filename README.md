## Behind the Design
When most people imagine their wedding, I doubt they're thinking about font choices, logo designs and websites. But for Megan and I — a studio artist and printmaker turned teacher, and a graphic designer with enough front-end knowledge to be dangerous — it's been top-of-mind since before I popped the question. The idea of distilling our life together down to a bunch of design decisions always seemed surprisingly fun. And, so far, doing just that has been deeply rewarding; it's been a new way for us to put our relationship (both where it's been and where it's going) in context, and see it in a whole new light. /pos

If I had to describe Megan and myself, I'd use words like Bohemian... eclectic... terms like "outdoorsy," maybe even "crunchy"? Hippies? I proposed on a mountaintop, for chrissakes. Anyway, the point is: Megan and I are deeply emotional, progressive and artistic people who laugh at the same silly jokes, cry at the same sentimental movies, sing to the same songs and dance to the same irregular drumbeat. Coming as no surprise, one of the very first things we bonded over when becoming friends was our love for art history, particularly the Impressionist and early American Realism movements. When I got to brainstorming how we'd like our wedding to look and feel, I figured the direction was staring us right in the face...

Our visual identity is deeply informed by the work of Claude Monet and the Impressionists: a focus on visible mark-making, preference for open-air compositions and unique viewpoints, depictions of every-day subject matter and how light expressed on the canvas (or the page, or the screen) makes you feel. This, combined with some of our other shared traits — a belief in free and open access to information and knowledge; our tendency toward finding the sacred in the mundane, _giving a shit_ about all the little parts of life; the at-times _jarring_ juxtaposition between our wild, warm and loud personalities and our moody, contemplative reflections of ourselves and the world — helped me to arrive at a handful of core principles for the identity:
* __It has to be human-made.__ We cherish work done by hand; just as Impressionism is defined by the brushstroke, so too should any depiction of our live and love feel like it's been crafted by our hands. This can be taken literally — handwritten touches, using actual Impressionist paintings in the design — but it can also be taken to a more basal level: the HTML/CSS is written by hand, the fonts are enthusiastically humanist selections, every piece of design is poured over with love and respect.
* __It should feel elegant, Bohemian. Never stuffy.__ Weddings are all about pomp and circumstance. So we figure, might as well go all in. Premium paper stocks, real florals and natural decor root the identity in real materials; compositions follow that Impressionist ideal of openness, with lots of negative space and room for every element to breathe; typography is cutting-edge but rooted in traditional elegance, and refactored for a modern wedding; the identity should feel fashionable and not like an expectation.
* __It has to reflect the natural beauty of upstate New York.__ We were born and raised in upstate New York (although, technically I was born in Vermont? Whatever. Same diff.) Oswego and Syracuse has been our home for a decade. For us, nothing beats a bike ride along the Erie Canal, a long-weekend escape to the ADK, swimming in the St. Lawrence River or hiking the Chimney Bluffs. Any identity that's a reflection of us has to be a reflection of the environment we've been fortunate enough to thrive in: in colors, in rhythm, in appearance, in attitude.

### Typography

Typographically, the serif font [Larken](https://ellenlufftype.com/larken/) — inspired by the organic curves and soft beauty of the natural environment, and one of my favorite fonts probably _ever_ — is the leading voice, giving the identity that defining elegance. Larken is paired with two strong sans-serifs rooted in history and modernity: 
* [Jost](https://indestructibletype.com/Jost.html), a sharp geometric sans named for the German typographer Heinrich Jost, who inspired Paul Renner to develop Futura; and
* [Public Sans](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Public+Sans/about), the open-source (former) official typeface of the United States Web Design System, based on Franklin Gothic, which is considered the patriarch of American sans-serif typefaces.

### Colors

Reflecting the beauty of upstate New York, with some nods to Monet's paintings in the mix, our identity's color palette features rich earthy tones punctuated by more vibrant accents. 

The core palette consists of a range of coffee-inspired brown tones, natural greens and floral purples, with a neutral off-white providing warmth and contrast. The twist of the palette, for me, is in the two accent tones — aptly-named "Sunflower" and "Vermilion" — which bring this impact, vibrancy and intrigue and compliment the more basal tones. When these colors mix and combine, I think you can really feel that Impressionist sensibility of depictions of light.

### Imagery

We'd be fools not to include Monet paintings in a visual identity inspired by Impressionism. Thankfully, the National Gallery of Art in Washington provides high-resolution photographs of the paintings from their collection _for free_ (as well as literally thousands of other artworks; the joy of public access!). We've selected some of our faves to use as background treatments throughout the identity.

As we start work with our (many!) photographer friends to do things like schedule engagement photoshoots and plan for the big day, photography for the identity will tend toward those same Impressionist principles; golden-hour, dappled light, warm tones and almost dreamlike abstractions will be the goal, while also drawing from the hills and valleys that surround our venue and define upstate New York.
