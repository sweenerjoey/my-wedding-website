## Behind the Design
When most people imagine their wedding, I doubt they're thinking about font choices, logo designs and websites. But for Megan and I — a printmaker-turned-teacher, and a graphic designer with enough front-end knowledge to be dangerous — it's been top-of-mind since before I popped the question. The idea of distilling our life together down to a bunch of design decisions has always struck me as a fun exercise. Doing just that has been surprisingly, deeply rewarding; it's become a new way for us to put our relationship (both where it's been and where it's going) into a new context, and see it in a refreshing new light.

If I had to describe me and Megan, I'd probably use words like "eclectic"... "outdoorsy?" Maybe even "crunchy?" We're not exactly _hippies_, but we're definitely a bit free-flowing (I proposed on a mountaintop, for chrissakes). The point being: we're deeply emotional, progressive and artistic people who laugh at the same dumb jokes, cry at the same dumb movies, sing to the same dumb songs and dance to the same irregular drumbeat. Coming as no surprise, one of the first things we ever bonded over when becoming friends was our love for art history, particularly the Impressionist and early American Realism movements. When I got to brainstorming how we'd like our wedding to look and feel, the direction was basically staring us right in the face...

### Visual Identity

Our visual identity is deeply informed by the work of Claude Monet and the Impressionists: visible mark-making, preference for open-air compositions and unique viewpoints, depictions of every-day subject matter and how light expressed on the canvas (or the page, or the screen) makes you feel. This, combined with our other shared convictions — a belief in free and open access to information and knowledge; our tendency toward finding the sacred among the mundane; _giving a shit_ about the little parts of life; the at-times _jarring_ juxtaposition of our boisterous personalities with our moodier, more contemplative reflections of ourselves and the world — helped me to arrive at a handful of core principles for the identity:
* __It has to be human-made.__ We cherish work done by hand; just as Impressionism is defined by the brushstroke, any rendition of our life and love put to paper needs to feel like it's been crafted by human hands. This can play out literally — handwritten touches, using actual Impressionist paintings in the design — but it can also be taken to a more basal conclusion: the HTML/CSS is written by hand, every piece of design is poured over with the love, respect and care of a curator, etc.
* __It should feel elegant, Bohemian. Never stuffy.__ We all know that weddings are about the pomp and circumstance. That doesn't mean it has to be boring, though: the identity can incorporate more eclectic, chic touches without feeling like we should be sitting in church pews. Premium paper stocks, real florals and natural decor root the identity in raw materials; compositions follow an Impressionist ideal of the open air, with lots of negative space and room for elements to breathe; typography is cutting-edge but rooted in a timeless elegance, refactored for a modern wedding; the identity should be fashionable, and not like an expectation.
* __It has to reflect the natural beauty of upstate New York.__ We were born and raised in upstate New York (although, technically I was born in Vermont? Whatever. Same diff.) Life along Lake Ontario is what we've known for the last decade. Nothing beats a bike ride along the Erie Canal, a long-weekend escape to the ADK, swimming in the St. Lawrence River, or hiking the Chimney Bluffs. Any identity that's a reflection of us has to be a reflection of the environment we've been fortunate enough to thrive in: through colors, in rhythm, in appearance, or in attitude.

### Typography

Typographically, the serif font [Larken](https://ellenlufftype.com/larken/) — inspired by the organic curves and soft beauty of the natural environment, and probably one of my favorite fonts _ever_ — is the leading voice, giving the identity that defining elegance I'm looking for. Larken is paired with two strong sans-serifs rooted in history and modernity: 
* [Jost](https://indestructibletype.com/Jost.html), a sharp geometric sans named for the German typographer Heinrich Jost, who inspired Paul Renner to develop Futura. Jost is angular and fashionable, and looks particularly good in all-caps treatments.
* [Public Sans](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Public+Sans/about), the open-source (former) official typeface of the United States Web Design System. Public Sans is based on Franklin Gothic, considered the patriarch of American sans-serif typefaces, and is a total workhorse of a font: strong rhythms, consistent letters, and legible across long runs of overly-long Joey words (LOL). To borrow a phrase originally used to describe the font Gotham, and apply it here: "it's what letters look like."

Taken together, this package of fonts feels exactly like Megan and I: consistent, with an edge. Logical with the artisanal. Perfectly staid in some aspects, and delightfully surprising in others. A sumtotal of rigid, mechanical precision and free-flowing, natural beauty.

### Colors

Reflecting the beauty of upstate New York, with some nods to Monet's paintings in the mix, our identity's color palette features rich earthy tones punctuated by surprisingly vibrant accents.

The core palette consists of a range of coffee-inspired brown tones, natural greens and floral purples, with a neutral off-white providing warmth and contrast. The twist of the palette, for me, is in the two accent tones — aptly-named "Sunflower" and "Vermilion" — which bring this impact, vibrancy and intrigue and compliment the baseline tones. When these colors mix and combine, I think you can really feel that Impressionist sensibility of light and shadow taking shape.

### Imagery

We'd be fools not to include Monet paintings in a visual identity inspired by Impressionism. Thankfully, the National Gallery of Art in Washington provides high-resolution photographs of the paintings from their collection _for free_ (as well as literally thousands of other artworks; the joy of public access!). We've selected some of our faves to use as background treatments throughout the identity.

As we start work with our (many!) photographer friends to do things like schedule engagement photoshoots and plan for the big day, photography for the identity will tend toward those same Impressionist principles; golden-hour, dappled light, warm tones and almost dreamlike abstractions will be the goal, while also drawing from the hills and valleys that surround our venue and define upstate New York.
